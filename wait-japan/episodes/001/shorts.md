# Episode 001 — Shorts Pack

Each Short uses a different curiosity mechanism so the channel does not feel mass-produced.

## Short 001A — The Myth
**Hook:** Think Japan removed all its trash cans after the 1995 subway attack? Not exactly.

**Script:**
Tokyo did remove railway-station trash cans after the 1995 sarin attack. But here's the part most explanations skip: the bins came back a few years later. Tokyo's metropolitan government says the larger wave of removals began in 2004, after train bombings in Europe increased security concerns. So 1995 is part of the story — not the whole story. And Tokyo was already changing its litter habits decades before either event.

**End card:** So why is Tokyo still so clean? Full story on WAIT, JAPAN?

## Short 001B — The Historical Reversal
**Hook:** Tokyo wasn't always this clean.

**Script:**
In the early 1950s, Tokyo had garbage lying on roads and trash polluting rivers. In 1954 the city launched a major cleanup campaign, and the push intensified before the 1964 Olympics. Waste systems improved, public campaigns expanded, and expectations slowly changed. The spotless Tokyo tourists know today wasn't simply inherited. It was built over decades.

**End card:** Clean Tokyo was made, not born. WAIT, JAPAN?

## Short 001C — The Tourist Test
**Hook:** In Tokyo, what do you do when there's no trash can?

**Script:**
Tokyo's official tourism guide says public areas and parks have relatively few garbage cans. Its advice is simple: carry a small garbage bag and take your waste back to your accommodation if necessary. That sounds inconvenient, but it reveals the bigger system behind Tokyo's clean streets: if you can't dispose of something properly now, you keep it with you.

**End card:** Next question: why are vending machines everywhere?
