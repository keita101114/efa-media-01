# Episode 001 Fact Sheet

## Working title
Why Does Japan Have So Few Trash Cans — Yet Stay So Clean?

## Editorial thesis
Do not explain Tokyo's cleanliness as a timeless national trait or as a one-event consequence of the 1995 sarin attack. The strongest official Tokyo source frames it as a long historical process involving waste infrastructure, policy, civic campaigns, security decisions, and changing social norms.

## Verified claims
1. Early-1950s Tokyo had serious litter and waste-management problems; household garbage could remain on roads and rivers were polluted with trash.
2. Tokyo Metropolitan Government launched a major Keep the City Clean initiative in 1954.
3. Tokyo's Olympic-era beautification drive expanded cleanup, waste collection and public-etiquette efforts.
4. Railway-station bins were removed after the 1995 sarin attack but returned a few years later.
5. TMG says mass elimination of station bins began in 2004 following train bombings in Europe.
6. Official Tokyo tourism guidance says public areas and parks have few garbage cans and advises visitors to carry a small bag and bring waste back to their accommodation when necessary.
7. Current Tokyo policy continues to promote taking waste home, sorting, community cleanup and recycling infrastructure.

## Primary sources
- Tokyo Metropolitan Government — Clean Tokyo Was Not Built in a Day: https://www.english.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/w/000-101-010900
- GO TOKYO — How to be a responsible traveler in Tokyo: https://www.gotokyo.org/en/plan/responsible-traveler/index.html
- Tokyo Metropolitan Government — KEEP TOKYO CLEAN: https://keep-tokyo-clean.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/

## Claims to avoid
- Japan has no trash cans.
- All trash cans disappeared in 1995 and never came back.
- Japanese people are naturally cleaner than other nationalities.
- The 1995 attack caused Tokyo to become clean.
- Any precise nationwide trash-can count without a primary source.

## Story engine
Visible paradox → common explanation → historical reversal → system explanation → present-day caveat → next question.
